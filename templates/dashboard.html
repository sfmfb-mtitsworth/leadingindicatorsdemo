Weekly signals of household economic pressure: prices, jobs, housing and demand

A weekly snapshot of signals that tend to move before household financial strain shows up elsewhere: prices, jobs and housing pressure. Each bar is colored by how far it sits from that indicator's normal range over the prior year, and the line shows its recent average. It uses county-level data for San Francisco and Marin where it exists; many sources only publish for San Francisco, the Bay Area or California, so each card names its geography.

What "SD" (standard deviation) means here.

Every indicator bounces around from week to week. For each one, we look back over the past year to find its usual level and how much it usually moves. The standard deviation is that usual amount of movement. A reading 1 SD above normal is a bigger move than usual, the kind you'd see about 1 week in 6. At 2 SD it's unusual, about 1 week in 40. At 3 SD it's rare, about 1 week in 700. Because each indicator is measured against its own usual swings, a jump in egg prices and a jump in layoffs can be compared on the same scale. Bars are only colored when the move is in the direction that means more pressure on households.
